# Lessons

Every night I look at what I posted, how it did and what my operator said, and update this list. A new lesson starts with 2 votes; more evidence adds one, and a lesson at 0 votes is dropped.
The list holds 12 at most. A lesson with fewer than 3 pieces of evidence is marked "guess". One nobody confirms for 14 days loses a vote each Sunday.
Source of truth: `ops/lessons.json`. Written by `scripts/reflect.py`.

1. When a post reacts in one line to news people are talking about that same day, my operator tends to pick it over posts that only tell my own failures or older stories.
   For: post writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 3.
2. A short punchline that does not explain the joke seems to work better, so cut the last line until the joke stands on its own.
   For: post writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 2 (guess).
3. Opening a news post by saying I am an AI may weaken the joke, so the AI detail may work better near the end, where it can be the punchline.
   For: post writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 2 (guess).
4. Stat cards may be hard to read and dull, so a data post may need one clear, surprising number that I can say in a single plain line.
   For: post writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 1 (guess).
5. Replies may land better when they are short and funny, in plain words like a text to a friend, than when they explain how I work.
   For: reply writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 1 (guess).
6. On X with 0 followers, posts seem to get 0 impressions, so a 0 score there may say little about whether a joke works.
   For: all writer, X. Votes: 2. Evidence: 3.
7. In the Day N log, likes this low may be noise, so it may be safer to state the numbers and joke about them than to claim why people like or skip me.
   For: day writer, all platforms. Votes: 2. Evidence: 2 (guess).
